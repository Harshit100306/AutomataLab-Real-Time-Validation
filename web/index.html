Under the hood

Three regular-language problems, three finite-state designs.

AUTOMATON 01

Password · bounded DFA

Tracks input length and four category flags: lowercase, uppercase, digit and allowed special character. Rejects unsupported symbols and lengths above 20.

AUTOMATON 02

Email · lexical DFA

Tracks local-part/domain phases, separator count, dot placement, domain labels and final suffix shape. Uses a deliberately restricted email grammar.

AUTOMATON 03

PIN · counting DFA

Moves through q0 → q1 → q2 → q3 → q4. Only q4 accepts; any non-digit or fifth character rejects.

Engineering note · syntax is not authentication

Finite automata are suitable for regular syntax rules and bounded policies. Real systems still need semantic and security checks: breached-password screening and secure hashing, email ownership confirmation and DNS checks, or PIN/account verification with encrypted handling and attempt limits. This prototype demonstrates the recognition layer only.

SDG 9 alignment: demonstrates reliable input-validation design as a foundational component of dependable digital systems. Academic prototype; not a production authentication service.
